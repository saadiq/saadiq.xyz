Update · 25 September 2026

First fixes are liveinitiative 1

We have released the first set of fixes to the live site and checked them there. The rest of this report describes the site as we found it; items marked FIXED are now resolved.

Fixed and live

  • Forms on phones. Test-drive, quote, service and parts forms stack to one column again, with full-width fields and readable labels.
  • Find a Dealer. The dealer list and map load again.
  • Lead email bugs. A double-clicked submit no longer sends a duplicate lead, including on the customer opt-in form. A customer message containing '&' or '<' no longer breaks the lead email. Quote answers the form already collected (timeframe, payment method, preferred contact, best time, company, address) now reach the sales team instead of being dropped. The emails' format, recipients and addresses are unchanged.

Still open in initiative 1

  • Remove the homepage slide that links to the lapsed toyotajamaica360.com domain. It is still live. This is a content change we can make right away.
  • Restrict the Google Maps key to the site's domain, and add a plain-text fallback so branch details show even if the map fails.
  • Clearer recall results, with Call and Book buttons.
  • Move lead recipients out of code into an admin setting.

Sales improvements to plan with your sales team

  • Lead reference details. Each lead email carries a malformed date and the same reference number. We want to confirm with whoever manages the receiving system how these fields are used before we change them.
  • Model and trim on every lead. Carry the model the shopper was viewing into the form (initiative 8).
  • Shorter forms and customer confirmations. Fewer required fields, a thank-you page, and an email or SMS confirmation with expected response times (initiative 8).
  • One pipeline for every lead type. Service, parts, pre-owned, trade-in and fleet leads currently go to separate inboxes. Route them all to your sales system with campaign source attached, plus response-time alerts (initiative 14).
2 · What's working

Keep and build on these

  • Price transparency. Every model shows a "From USD" price in the menu and on its model page. Kia Jamaica and Hyundai Jamaica show none.
  • Desktop vehicle menu. Photos, prices and body-type tabs for all 14 models, one hover away.
  • Local storytelling. The 2026 RAV4 creative was shot at Emancipation Park and in the Blue Mountains. Kia Jamaica's homepage uses generic global imagery.
  • Certified pre-owned. Listings are clean, priced in J$, with mileage, branch and a working inline enquiry.
  • Recall lookup and live chat. We found neither on the Jamaican competitor sites we surveyed.
  • Solid base. HTTPS, Cloudflare in front, current WordPress core, an SEO plugin already in place.
  • A sister site to borrow from. Toyota Trinidad & Tobago, also a Toyota Tsusho company, runs on an almost identical template. It already has a finance calculator we could adapt.

7 · Market context

Where Toyota Jamaica stands

  • The main rival is often a used import, not another brand. Jamaica imported about three used vehicles for every new one in 2023 (Gleaner, Jan 2024). Banks lend on better terms for new cars, but the site never makes that case.
  • Chinese brands and EVs are arriving fast. ATL is BYD's Caribbean master dealer and also sells MG and Omoda/Jaecoo. Stewart's sells GWM Haval. Toyota's hybrid range is its answer, but the site's hybrid page is a generic explainer.
  • Fleet matters. Rental companies buy roughly a quarter of new vehicles in Jamaica (Observer, Jan 2025). The fleet page is thin, and its menu item is hidden.
  • Hurricane season. After Hurricane Melissa (Oct 2025), one insurer reported motor claims up about 30%. Stewart's ran a relief pledge. Toyota Jamaica's site offers no insurance-claim or replacement path.
  • Regional Toyota sites are ahead on buying tools. Costa Rica has a step-by-step quote builder with a maintenance plan and WhatsApp. Panama features trade-in and KINTO leasing. Trinidad & Tobago has a finance calculator and a maintenance plan.

    Method

    We reviewed the live site on an emulated iPhone 13 and a 1440px desktop. We ran Lighthouse and accessibility scans, crawled links and sitemaps, and read the theme and plugin code. We benchmarked against Jamaican dealer sites and regional Toyota distributors. We did not submit any forms. A second reviewer spot-checked the key claims against the live site before publication. Market figures cite their published source. We had no access to your analytics or lead data, so all impact scores are estimates, to be recalibrated once measurement is in place (initiative 3).
